Example trade ticket

Action Buy to open · Quantity 1 put contract · Underlying NVDA · Strike $180 · Limit premium $6.00 per share

If filled at $6.00, the buyer pays 1 × 100 × $6 = $600 upfront. A trader using Sell to open receives that $600 and assumes the put seller’s obligation. Two contracts cover 200 shares and cost $1,200.

The premium quote is per share. The order quantity is contracts. A standard, unadjusted equity-option contract covers 100 shares. Total premium = quoted premium × 100 × number of contracts. The premium is paid once when opening this position.

A call buyer can buy 100 shares at the strike. A put buyer can sell 100 shares at the strike. If assigned, a call seller must deliver 100 shares and a put seller must buy 100 shares. At a $180 strike, exercise of one contract involves $18,000 and 100 shares. This exercise payment is separate from the premium.

Market convention and classroom assumptions

Standard U.S. equity options are generally American-style and physically settled. We evaluate economic payoffs at expiration, assume exercise when in the money, and ignore early exercise, dividends, commissions, taxes, interest and margin requirements. An option can also be closed in the market before expiration. Adjusted contracts can have a different deliverable. This case uses standard 100-share contracts throughout.

Formula help · payoff versus P&L

Buyer call payoff = max(final stock price − strike, 0) × 100 × contracts.
Buyer put payoff = max(strike − final stock price, 0) × 100 × contracts.
Buyer P&L = payoff − total premium paid.
Seller payoff = negative of buyer payoff. Seller P&L = premium received − buyer payoff.

For sellers, enter a negative option payoff when the position creates an obligation. These are economic option values at expiration. A buyer’s option premium does not include the cash needed to buy shares on exercise.

Scenario A · A payment in three months

The client owns the 100 NVDA shares and has $600 available for the put premium. At expiration the client needs at least $17,400 for a family payment and would be uncomfortable with a large stock loss.

Refer to 100 shares, one put contract, $600 premium, the terminal floor and the payment deadline.

Scenario B · Ten years of investing

The client holds the same 100 shares for a ten-year goal, has a separate emergency fund and accepts temporary stock losses. They propose buying a new three-month put repeatedly. Future put premiums are unknown.

Refer to investment horizon, repeated premiums and when protection is needed. The current put protects only until this expiration. Justify your recommendation using the client’s needs.
