Your role · Wealth Management Advisor

Today you advise two clients on their investment portfolios. Your job is to turn financial data into clear, practical recommendations that fit each client’s goals.

You will guide them over the next three years as portfolio values, market interest rates and cash needs evolve. Explain what changes in value mean, distinguish income from investment returns, and help each client decide whether to hold, sell or adjust their portfolio. Most calculations are provided; focus on your analysis and conversations with the clients.

Simplifying convention for the entire minicase: each bond pays one coupon per year, not semiannually. All Excel formulas use annual yields, annual coupon amounts and years to maturity. Client A: you choose whether coupons stay in cash at 0%, earn the market short-term rate, or are reinvested in SPY. This is part of your strategy; the cash reserve earns 0%. Client B: coupons are saved in cash at 0% for the separate return-analysis exercise.

Client A · Fund the family commitments

“I have $250,000 today. I need $80,000 in two years for my son’s college expenses and $120,000 in three years toward my daughter’s mortgage. I want investment growth, but I must be able to make both payments.”

Recommend an allocation to five hypothetical U.S. Treasury bonds, an S&P 500 investment and cash. Each expense is a single nominal payment. No new deposits, taxes, fees, short selling or borrowing. Choose whole-bond quantities and a cash reserve; all remaining capital automatically goes to SPY. Fractional SPY shares are allowed. Choose a coupon policy: cash at 0%, market-rate reinvestment, or SPY. Explain why it suits the client’s payment needs. The cash reserve earns 0%. Payments occur just after that year’s coupons and principal arrive.

Client B · Understand the quoted YTM

“SPY’s recent performance has disappointed me, and I am uncomfortable with its volatility. I want to invest in a CCC corporate bond offering a high YTM. I have decided to buy it. Help me understand: is that quoted yield fixed, and what should I expect over the next three years?”

The client is committed to purchasing the bond. Your role is to clarify the return expectations and guide the client through annual coupon income, changes in market value and the final maturity payment. Your assigned coupon rate and initial YTM are specific to your individual case; assume all annual coupons and principal are paid. Each year, separate realized coupon income from the change in bond value, then explain the total to the client. At maturity, compare final wealth growth with the initial YTM. Most figures are provided. The client’s view of SPY is a personal assessment in this hypothetical scenario.

Instructor methodology and grading

All students face the same 10,000 scenarios and the same selling rules. Primary ranking: lowest percentage of scenarios with any missed payment. Tie-breaker: highest mean annualized time-weighted return. Also report mean wealth after both payments and average unpaid amount when a strategy fails.

Simulation assumptions and fair comparison

Three annual steps. Equity total returns follow lognormal returns with 7% expected continuous growth parameter and 18% volatility. Treasury yields receive independent normal parallel shocks with 1 percentage point annual volatility, floored at zero. Equity and rate shocks are independent in this teaching model. No bond default. Cash earns 0%. Client A’s coupons follow the student’s selected cash, market-rate or SPY policy; maturing principal stays in cash. These are chosen assumptions, not estimates from the historical charts.

At each year-end: apply the year’s market returns to existing holdings, route new coupons according to the selected policy at that time, receive maturing principal in cash, then make the scheduled payment. Newly reinvested coupons do not receive the year’s earlier stock return. Use cash first, then the liquid market-rate coupon account if selected; if insufficient, sell the same fraction of every remaining bond and equity holding. If assets are insufficient, record the unpaid balance. Never borrow or hide a missed payment. There is no discretionary rebalancing.

Time-weighted annualized returns remove the effect of actual withdrawals. A 0% simulated failure rate is not a universal guarantee. Exact maturity matching covers the nominal bills under the stated no-default assumptions. The Monte Carlo ranking is separate from the academic grade.
