Your assignment: resolve the valuation debate

For this case, assume the market is divided on NVIDIA’s fair value: bullish investors argue Buy because AI demand can sustain growth, while bearish investors argue Sell because the price may already reflect overly optimistic expectations. These are the case’s opposing positions, not a survey of current analyst ratings.

Your job: prepare a market valuation report with an estimated fair value per share and a justified Buy, Hold or Sell recommendation. Use fundamentals, DCF, two valuation multiples and technical evidence to explain which position you support.

NVIDIA supplies GPUs, networking systems and software used in AI training and inference. Its platform combines hardware with the CUDA software ecosystem. The business relies on external manufacturing and on customers continuing to invest in computing infrastructure.

Growth case

Revenue rose from $130.5bn in FY2025 to $215.9bn in FY2026. First-half FY2027 revenue reached $177.8bn, versus $90.8bn a year earlier. These figures support strong demand, but do not establish a sustainable five-year growth rate.

What can challenge the thesis?

Customer concentration, export restrictions, manufacturing capacity and competing chips can affect growth and margins. A successful company can still be an expensive stock if its price assumes exceptionally strong future results.

        02 Your FCFF valuation

        15 min

        From Lecture 8's FCFE to this case's FCFF

        Lecture 8 valued cash available to shareholders using FCFE and the cost of equity, Ke. This case extends that logic to cash available to debt and equity investors together: FCFF, discounted at WACC. The worksheet handles the calculations; use this guide to understand them.

        Match the cash flow, discount rate and claim
        Lecture 8: FCFEThis case: FCFF
        Start withNet income, after interest and taxEBIT × (1 − T), before financing costs
        Reinvestment+ D&A − Capex − ΔWC+ D&A − Capex − ΔWC
        Debt financing+ Net debt issuedNo borrowing or repayment in FCFF
        Discount rateKe: cost of equityWACC: cost of debt and equity capital
        Value obtainedEquity value directly; do not subtract debt againOperating enterprise value; bridge to equity
        Worked bridge: FCFE → FCFF, using a small example

        Illustrative company, $m: EBIT 120, interest 20, tax 20%, D&A 10, capex 25, ΔWC 5 and net borrowing 8. NI = (120 − 20) × 80% = 80.

        FCFE = 80 + 10 − 25 − 5 + 8 = 68.
        FCFF = 120 × 80% + 10 − 25 − 5 = 76.

        Equivalently: FCFF = FCFE + interest × (1 − T) − net borrowing = 68 + 16 − 8 = 76. This assumes the simplified income statement used in class; it is not NVIDIA's reported accounting bridge.

        WACC = E/(D+E) × Ke + D/(D+E) × Kd × (1−T), using market-value financing weights. We provide 12% as a teaching assumption, so you do not need to estimate WACC. Use Ke only for FCFE; use WACC only for FCFF.

        Choose two assumptions. The worksheet projects revenue and operating profit, then calculates FCFF and discounts it at the WACC. These are your group's estimates, not company guidance.

        Enterprise value (EV) here means the value of operating assets available to debt and equity investors. Discounting FCFF at WACC gives this operating value; add nonoperating cash and investments and subtract financial debt to obtain equity value. “Asset value” alone can be confused with accounting total assets.

        Which growth rate?

        You choose sales growth for years 1–5 below. Terminal g describes sustainable growth from year 6 onward, not recent NVIDIA sales growth. For FCFF, g = reinvestment rate × ROIC. The supplied stable assumptions imply 15% × 20% = 3%. This is a model assumption, not an observed or guaranteed growth rate. Lecture 8's equity equivalent is g = ERR × ROE. Do not carry a high five-year growth rate into perpetuity.

        FCFF = EBIT × (1 − T) + D&A − Capex − ΔNWC
        Formula help: follow the worksheet from sales to value per share
        1. Sales: prior-year sales × (1 + your growth rate). EBIT: sales × your operating margin.
        2. NOPAT: EBIT × (1 − tax rate). It is operating profit after tax, before financing costs.
        3. Reinvestment: add back noncash D&A, subtract capex and the increase in operating working capital. As in Lecture 8, exclude cash and financial debt from working capital.
        4. Present value: year-t FCFF / (1 + WACC)t. Discount the terminal value back five years too, following Lecture 7's two-stage timing.
        5. Equity bridge: operating EV + nonoperating cash and investments − financial debt. Divide by diluted shares, as in Lecture 9's EV/EBITDA example.

        The formulas use USD millions and shares in millions, so the final result is USD per share. The spreadsheet and website use the same formulas.

        Common teaching assumptions and terminal value

        WACC 12%; tax 18%; D&A 1.2% of sales; capex 3% of sales; operating NWC 15% of sales. ΔNWC is 15% of the increase in sales, not 15% of total sales.

        From year 6, growth normalizes to 3%, operating margin to 45% and ROIC to 20%. ROIC is the return on invested debt-and-equity capital. This extends Lecture 8's relationship g = equity reinvestment rate × ROE: for operating cash flows, g = total reinvestment rate × ROIC. Therefore, the stable reinvestment rate is 3% / 20% = 15% of NOPAT.

        Terminal FCFF = year-6 NOPAT × (1 − 15%). TV at year 5 = terminal FCFF / (12% − 3%). This includes reinvestment needed to sustain growth. All terminal assumptions are supplied; the transition is a classroom simplification.

        Equity value = enterprise value + cash + investments − financial debt. Reported investments are added at carrying value; the model does not forecast investment revaluations, acquisitions, buybacks or dilution. The 24,285 million diluted share count is a fixed classroom proxy.

        04 Technical evidence

        5 min

        RSI (14)

        Wilder RSI summarizes recent gains versus losses. Above 70 is commonly called overbought; below 30, oversold. These readings do not guarantee a reversal.

        MACD (12, 26, 9)

        MACD = EMA12 − EMA26. The signal is its 9-session EMA; histogram = MACD − signal. A positive histogram means MACD is above its signal, and a negative one means below. A crossover requires a change of sign across dates; today's sign alone does not prove a new crossover.

        Both indicators are calculated from the same daily closes. EMA starts with a simple average of its initial window; the signal starts with the first nine valid MACD values. Neither indicator establishes intrinsic value. The moving averages remain background context on the price chart.

        Key concepts to address: RSI level and 30/70 thresholds; MACD versus signal; histogram sign; timing versus intrinsic value; whether the signals agree. Minimum 50 characters.
